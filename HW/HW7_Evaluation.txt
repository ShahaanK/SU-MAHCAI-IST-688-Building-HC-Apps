3a. ARCHITECTURE

The CSV has 1,290 rows about 177 clients, published July 30 to August 6, 2024.
Whole company blocks repeat, so I kept one article per client and URL, leaving
1,090. HW7_build_db.py does the heavy work offline and commits the result, and
the app only loads it (0.01 seconds).

The RAG store is text-embedding-3-small vectors in a NumPy array plus JSON
metadata, searched by cosine similarity. With 1,090 vectors an index adds
nothing, and a committed Chroma folder could break on a newer chromadb on
Streamlit Cloud. Same-client articles with similarity of 0.78 or more count as
one story (861 stories), so a list never repeats an event.

Similarity search can't find "most interesting", so gpt-5-mini read every
article once, offline, with a rubric for a law firm watching its clients. It
returns a 0-10 score (litigation, regulators, settlements, M&A and layoffs
score high), a one-line reason, and whether the article is about the client at
all. That flag removed 252 name collisions, such as Da Vinci under VINCI and a
garden caterpillar under Caterpillar.

The LLM picks one of two tools. rank_news sorts by score, then outlets
covering the story, then date, and the stored reason supplies each story's
context. search_news runs the vector search with an optional client filter
(Meta maps to Facebook). A second LLM call answers only from the tool output.
Memory keeps 5 turns.

3b. HOW I KNOW THE RANKING IS GOOD

Before any scoring, I wrote a gold set from the CSV: 15 stories a law firm
must see, such as Intel's 15,000 layoffs and the Google antitrust ruling, and
13 noise items.

  Must-see stories in top 10 / 15 / 20:   7 / 10 / 12 of 15
  Noise items that rank at all:           0 of 13

The top 7 are all must-see. JPMorgan's CFPB story ranks only #50, and two
Walmart crime stories, one a 2019 shooting retrospective, scored 10 and ranked
#8 and #11. The cause is score compression: 67 articles scored 9 or 10, so
outlet count breaks the ties.

I also ran 8 messages through the real page on both models, covering the
ranking, a memory follow-up, a clean client, a name collision, a topic, a
non-client (Boeing) and small talk. All passed except gpt-5-mini returning one
Nvidia story instead of a list. Testing caught two bugs I fixed: duplicate
URLs in search, and "$" amounts rendering as LaTeX. Next I'd have gpt-5.5
re-rank the top 40 in one call; just swapping in its scores was mixed (12
must-see stories in the top 20 became 11).


4. LOWER-COST VS HIGHER-COST LLM

I compared gpt-5-mini ($0.25 in / $2 out per million tokens) with gpt-5.5
($5 / $30), 20x and 15x the price.

As the scorer. gpt-5.5 rescored 100 articles with the same prompt: every
gold-set article, the two Walmart stories and a seeded random sample.

  Agree on "about the client"      94 of 100
  Mean score difference            0.83 points (82 of 100 within 1)
  Spearman rank correlation        0.917
  Top-10 overlap in the sample     4 of 10 (many ties at 9 and 10)
  Noise labeled off-topic          26 of 26 for both

Where they differed, gpt-5.5 usually judged better. It scored the El Paso
retrospective 2 instead of 10 ("limited new legal or business significance"),
the Walmart stabbing 8 instead of 10, and raised the JPMorgan CFPB and R1 RCM
stories from 8 to 9, the two the cheap ranking buried. It also marked down an
earnings-date notice with "no results ... yet." It was not better everywhere:
it scored Apple's pressure on Tencent 6 against 8, and the two disagreed on
three articles that may only name the client in passing.

As the chat model. Both answered all 8 test messages from the tool output
without inventing facts. gpt-5.5 chose better tool arguments (10 Nvidia
stories, not 1), named the CFPB story as JPMorgan's highest-stakes item, and
skipped an off-topic antitrust hit that gpt-5-mini included in one run. It
was also faster through OpenRouter: 43 seconds for the six-turn conversation
against 101 for gpt-5-mini, and 24 against 59 for the two-turn one.

Summary. The cheap model is good enough to label and score all 1,090
articles (about $0.20 and 19 minutes), and it agrees with gpt-5.5 94% of the
time on relevance. Its errors cluster at the top of the ranking, which is the
part a law firm reads. gpt-5.5 is the better chat model here: better
judgment, faster, and at this volume the price gap is a few cents a question.
The best split is mini for bulk scoring and gpt-5.5 for re-ranking the top
stories and for chat. Total OpenRouter spend for the assignment was $0.84.
